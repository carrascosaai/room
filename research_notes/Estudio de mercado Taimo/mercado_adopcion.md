# Market size, real adoption and retention of PFM / budgeting apps in Spain and Europe (as of Oct 2026)

Research note for "Taimo" (an automatic expense-tracking and budgeting app, launching in Spain first). Method: web search across about 18 queries. The WebFetch tool could not resolve several domains (sensortower.com, businessofapps.com, revenuecat.com, yapily.com), so some figures come from search-result summaries of those pages and not from the full pages. Each such case is flagged "(snippet-level)". Most of the market-size and retention figures come from secondary sources. Treat them as orders of magnitude, not facts.

## 1. Market size and growth (global / Europe / Spain)

### Takeaway
Published "personal finance app" market sizes disagree by more than 10x, from about USD 0.33B (Europe PFM software) to about USD 166B (global "personal finance apps"). The difference comes from category definitions, so none of these numbers can size Taimo's market. The usable signal is bottom-up: real budgeting-app revenue in Spain is tiny. Sensor Tower's Spain top-5 budgeting lists are led by apps with weekly revenue in the hundreds of USD.

### Cited Findings
- Global "personal finance apps" estimates for 2025 range from about USD 31.7B (CAGR about 20.8%) to about USD 114.9B (USD 311.9B by 2035) and USD 165.9B. All come from commercial report vendors with undisclosed or broad definitions — [The Business Research Company](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/personal-finance-apps-global-market-report); [Market Research Future](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/personal-finance-mobile-app-market-22409); [Research Nester](https://www.researchnester.com/reports/personal-finance-apps-market/8243); [CoinLaw roundup](https://coinlaw.io/personal-finance-app-industry-statistics/) (snippet-level)
- Global budgeting-apps market: about USD 6.8B in 2024, with a 9.5% CAGR for 2025–2033 — [DataHorizzon Research](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/budgeting-apps-market-47078) (snippet-level)
- Europe "financial app" market (which includes payments, lending and investing): USD 53.71B in 2025, forecast at USD 248.24B by 2033 (21.09% CAGR). This scope is far broader than budgeting — [Transpire Insight](https://www.transpireinsight.com/report/europe-financial-app-market) (snippet-level)
- Europe personal finance *software* market: about USD 0.33B in 2025. This much narrower definition shows how much the scope changes the figure — [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/personal-finance-software-market-112683) (snippet-level)
- Europe's share of the global PFM app market is put at about 25% by one source and about 30% by another. The sources do not agree — [Market Research Intellect](https://www.marketresearchintellect.com/product/personal-finance-app-market/); [CoinLaw](https://coinlaw.io/personal-finance-app-industry-statistics/) (snippet-level)
- Statista Market Insights, Spain "Finance" app outlook (old 2022 projection, flagged as dated): 2022 revenue of €3.79M, CAGR 14.63% for 2022–2027, giving about €8.2M in 2027. The figure is in-app/paid-app revenue only, so it excludes affiliate and bank income. It was retrieved through a library proxy — [Statista Finance – Spain](https://www.statista.com/outlook/amo/app/finance/spain/); [proxied page](https://es-statista-com.ezproxy.canberra.edu.au/outlook/dmo/app/finance/spain)
- Sensor Tower Spain, Q1 2025, "Personal Finance – Budgeting & Planning": Splitwise's weekly revenue peaked at about USD 861 and its active users ended the quarter just under 393K. Monefy, YNAB, Wallet and Spendee also appear in the Spanish top 5 — [Sensor Tower Q1 2025 ES revenue](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q1-unified-top-5-personal%20finance%20budgeting%20and%20planning-revenue-es-63e363f0e1714cfff1979caa); [Sensor Tower Q1 2025 ES units](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q1-unified-top-5-personal%20finance%20budgeting%20and%20planning-units-es-63e363f0e1714cfff1979caa) (snippet-level; the page could not be fetched)
- Sensor Tower also publishes a Europe top-5 list for budgeting app revenue in Q2 2025 (exact numbers not retrieved) — [Sensor Tower Europe Q2 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-unified-top-5-personal%20finance%20budgeting%20and%20planning-revenue-europe-63e363f0e1714cfff1979caa)
- Germany benchmark: Finanzguru, an open-banking PFM app, had more than 1.5M registered users. About 100K of them had bought brokerage, financial or insurance products through the app. Annualized revenue passed €10M in 2022, revenue tripled that year, and the company reached operational break-even before growth marketing. It raised €13M in April 2023, led by SCOR Ventures and PayPal Ventures, for €27M in total funding (2023 data, flagged as older) — [PayPal newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2023-04-04-PayPal-Ventures-Invests-in-Finanzguru); [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/finanzguru-bags-e13m-in-funding-led-by-scor-ventures-paypal/)
- Spain benchmark: Fintonic, the Spanish PFM app, raised €19M at a €160M valuation (earlier round). Reported user counts are inconsistent: about 400K or about 700K active users, with unclear dates. Fintonic reportedly reached profitability for the first time in summer 2025, after closing Mexico and Chile to refocus on Spain. It has partnered with BBVA Consumer Finance to sell loans, which shows a lending and marketplace monetization model — [El Referente (round)](https://elreferente.es/?p=18719); [Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/spanish-fintonic-app-raises-funds-at-e160m-valuation/); [Roams (CEO change)](https://roams.es/actualidad/finanzas/fintonic-nuevo-ceo-banco-santander-eneko-fonseca/); [El Referente (BBVA CF deal)](https://elreferente.es/?p=16633)
- US context: after Mint closed, Monarch Money raised a USD 75M Series B in May 2025 at a reported USD 850M post-money valuation. Third-party estimates (unverified) put Monarch at about 1.8M MAU and YNAB at about 1.4M MAU in early 2026, and YNAB revenue at about USD 49M (Growjo estimate) — [Sacra – Monarch](https://sacra.com/c/monarch-money/); [Luminix competitive landscape](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/competitive-landscape-personal-finance-and-budgeting-apps-2026/source/1)

### Inferences
- Use bottom-up sizing for Taimo, not top-down "market" reports. Spain has about 48M people. Paid budgeting-app revenue in Spain looks small: a single-digit € millions per year in the Statista 2022 projection, and top apps earning hundreds to low thousands of USD per week. Most of the money in Spanish PFM has come from lending and insurance referrals, as at Fintonic, not from subscriptions.
- The strongest European proofs of a business are Finanzguru (about €10M ARR at more than 1.5M registered users, mostly from product brokerage) and Fintonic (about a decade before first profitability). Both point to small revenue per user and a long path to break-even.

### Gaps
- I found no audited or primary figure for Spain-only or Europe-only budgeting-app revenue for 2024–2026. Statista's current Spain outlook and Sensor Tower's full numbers are paywalled or could not be fetched.
- I found no up-to-date Fintonic user or revenue figures; Registro Mercantil filings would be needed. I also found nothing current on Emma (UK) or other European players.

## 2. Actual adoption: share of Spaniards / Europeans using a budgeting or PFM app vs. bank tools

### Takeaway
I found no recent representative survey that measures dedicated budgeting-app use in Spain. The last specific data point (Ipsos, 2016) found that only about 4% of household money managers used free mobile apps and 0.1% used paid ones. Most people checked bank statements instead. Bank apps are where people actually look at their spending. The founder's "I don't know anyone who uses one" is consistent with the evidence available.

### Cited Findings
- Ipsos study "Control de Gastos mediante APPS" (Spain, published Nov 2017, fieldwork about 2016–17; OLD): most people who managed household finances used no app and kept reviewing bank statements. Only 4% used free mobile apps and 0.1% used paid tools. Among app users, "Tus gastos" had 9% use and Fintonic 6% — [Ipsos PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2017-11/np_%22Control%20de%20Gastos%20mediante%20APPS%22.pdf)
- BBVA Spain: about 6 in 10 digital customers use its financial-health and categorization tools. Satisfaction among those users is about 30% higher than among non-users (date unclear) — [Funds Society](https://www.fundssociety.com/es/?p=232335) (snippet-level)
- Spain's digital-banking base: about 69% of the population used online banking by 2023. An elEconomista piece projects 85% digital-banking use by 2027 (a forecast) — [Noda – Open Banking in Spain](https://noda.live/articles/open-banking-in-spain); [elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/actualidad/noticias/13015786/10/24/finanzas-a-golpe-de-clic-el-85-de-la-poblacion-espanola-usara-la-banca-digital-en-2027.html)
- Bizum has about 28–29M users, roughly half of Spain's adult population. It is the dominant fintech habit in Spain but is a payment app, not a budgeting app — [BBVA](https://www.bbva.com/es/salud-financiera/las-10-apps-para-gestionar-y-compartir-tus-gastos/); [Inpay](https://www.inpay.com/news-and-insights/open-banking-in-spain/)
- Spain's young adults: the Funcas survey of Oct 2026 (800 internet users aged 16–35) focuses on a rising "investor spirit", not budgeting apps. About 45% of young Spaniards follow financial influencers — [Funcas 2026](https://www.funcas.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2610-Encuesta-CF-jovenes-Final.pdf); [elEconomista](https://www.eleconomista.es/actualidad/noticias/13015786/10/24/finanzas-a-golpe-de-clic-el-85-de-la-poblacion-espanola-usara-la-banca-digital-en-2027.html)
- Europe: the most recent ING International Survey item I found on this topic is from Dec 2015 (OLD). About 26% of Europeans used mobile banking to monitor their finances, and 42% of those who improved their financial decisions said a detailed budget helped — [ING/Ipsos IIS PDF](https://think.ing.com/uploads/reports/IIS_TFI_Personal_Finance_Choices_That_Pay.pdf)
- US context (not Europe): only 21% of US consumers created or revised a budget in the past year (PYMNTS). Corporate Insight found 62% have created a budget, and 60% of Gen Z prefer digital budgeting apps — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/study_posts/consumers-say-they-want-budgeting-tools-but-arent-using-them/); [Corporate Insight](https://corporateinsight.com/survey-how-and-why-consumers-budget/)

### Inferences
- In Spain, dedicated budgeting-app use is probably in the low single-digit percent of adults, using the 2016 Ipsos baseline plus Sensor Tower's top app (Splitwise) at about 393K active users, which is a bill-splitter and not even a budgeting app. Taimo's real competitor for attention is the bank app (BBVA reports about 60% of digital customers using its tools), not other standalone apps.
- "Want to budget" is far from "use a tool". Even in the US, only about 1 in 5 consumers updates a budget each year.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 data on budgeting-app use from Banco de España, CNMV, Eurostat or OCU. The Banco de España / CNMV Encuesta de Competencias Financieras might have relevant items, but this search did not surface any.

## 3. Retention: typical D30/D90 for finance and budgeting apps, and why people abandon them

### Takeaway
Finance apps keep roughly 3–8% of installs by day 30 on most secondary benchmarks, with some sources quoting 10–25% for fintech. Digital banking apps retain better (about 11.6% at D30). Common reasons for abandoning budgeting apps are manual-entry fatigue, broken bank sync, miscategorization, and discouraging over-budget design. I found no rigorous survey that quantifies these reasons.

### Cited Findings
- Finance D30 retention is about 4.2% — [Business of Apps – Finance App Benchmarks 2026](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/finance-app-benchmarks/) (snippet-level)
- Finance D30 retention in 2025 is about 3% on Android and 3.1% on iOS — [Blockchain-ads](https://www.blockchain-ads.com/post/app-retention-benchmarks)
- Finance D30 retention is 8.03% — [GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/); fintech is 10–15%, against a cross-category median of about 4% — [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/)
- Higher estimates: 15–22% for finance and fintech ([Semnexus](https://semnexus.com/day-1-day-7-day-30-retention-benchmarks-app-category-2026)), and 15–25% for fintech at D30, attributed to AppsFlyer 2023 ([Apsteq](https://apsteq.com/blog/app-retention-benchmarks/)). These outliers are blog-level and inconsistent with the others
- Older vendor benchmarks: finance D30 was 5.8% (Statista) and 4.51% (AppsFlyer); digital banking D30 was 11.6%; overall D30 was 6.5% (Adjust) and 3.36% (AppsFlyer) — [Sendbird](https://sendbird.com/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-broken-down-by-industry)
- RevenueCat 2025: across subscription apps, monthly and annual retention is a known pain point, and longer trials (17–32 days) convert best at 45.7%. No finance-specific D90 figure was retrieved — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [RocketShip HQ summary](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/revenuecat-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-summary/)
- Reasons for abandonment (vendor and blog sources, no primary data): bank connections drop and data goes "weeks out of date"; manual entry becomes "a chore that gets skipped after three weeks"; categories are rigid or wrong; red "over budget" alerts make people avoid the app; privacy and cost concerns — [Gerald – expense tracking problems](https://joingerald.com/learn/financial-wellness/expense-tracking-apps-common-problems); [Gerald – money management apps](https://joingerald.com/learn/money-basics/money-management-apps-common-problems-guide); [Puelz Substack](https://robertpuelz.substack.com/p/no-budget-app-fix-now)
- A small qualitative study (University of Utah student project): some people tried budgeting apps but stopped because of usability difficulties — [Utah PDF](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5540/au16/samples/2d/2d_kaching.pdf)
- Mint was neglected for years ("the app has been abandoned for years", per a user quoted by CNBC). Shows that product quality decays without a monetization engine behind it — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html)

### Inferences
- If Taimo hits a typical finance D30 of about 4–8%, it needs about 12–25 installs for each user still active after a month. Automatic sync removes the manual-entry reason for leaving but adds dependence on PSD2 connection reliability, including the 90/180-day reconsent cycle (general PSD2 knowledge, not sourced here).
- The founder's experience ("I don't know anyone who uses one") fits low adoption plus very high churn. Many people have tried an app, but few keep using one.

### Gaps
- I found no budgeting-specific (as opposed to finance-wide) D30 or D90 figure from a primary source (AppsFlyer, Adjust or Sensor Tower pages were not reachable). I also found no Spain-specific retention data.
- I found no rigorous survey that quantifies reasons for abandonment in Europe.

## 4. Who uses these apps, and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Usage leans young and digital (Gen Z and millennials). Paid conversion is structurally low: in RevenueCat's 2025 benchmark, freemium apps convert about 2.2% of downloads to paid, against about 12.1% for hard paywalls. US budgeting subscriptions cost about USD 95–109 per year. The evidence from Spain (0.1% paying in 2016) suggests European willingness to pay is much lower.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025: median download-to-paid conversion is 12.1% for hard paywalls and 2.2% for freemium. Download-to-trial is 9.8% for high-priced apps and 4.3% for low-priced apps. More than 80% of trials start on day 0 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Singular summary](https://www.singular.net/blog/subscription-apps/); [RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/revenuecat-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-summary/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026: hard-paywall conversion fell by about 2 points from 12.1%, freemium held at about 2.1%, and download-to-paid is now measured at D35 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- US price points: YNAB charges USD 14.99/month or USD 109/year; Monarch USD 14.99/month or USD 99.99/year per household; Copilot USD 13/month or USD 95/year (iOS-first, and claimed but unverified at 100K+ subscribers) — [EnvelopeBudgeting comparison](https://envelopebudgeting.com/articles/monarch-vs-ynab); [Subger – Monarch](https://subger.com/en/service/monarch-money); [Unstar comparison](https://unstar.app/blog/ynab-rocket-money-monarch-everydollar-copilot-budget-apps-ranked-2026)
- Spain (OLD, 2016–17): only 0.1% of household money managers used paid tools — [Ipsos PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2017-11/np_%22Control%20de%20Gastos%20mediante%20APPS%22.pdf)
- Gen Z prefers digital budgeting apps (60% in the US survey) — [Corporate Insight](https://corporateinsight.com/survey-how-and-why-consumers-budget/). Spanish youth are more drawn to investing than to budgeting (Funcas 2026) — [Funcas](https://www.funcas.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2610-Encuesta-CF-jovenes-Final.pdf)
- In Spain, Bizum is the first choice for person-to-person payments among people under 45, and young people use mobile payments the most (Banco de España / Ipsos 2024 cash-habits study) — [Datatonics summary](https://datatonics.substack.com/p/418-estudio-sobre-habitos-en-el-uso)

### Inferences
- A rough illustration, not a forecast: with about 2% freemium conversion and a Spanish price of about €3–5/month (my assumption, below US levels), 100K Spanish downloads would give about 2K payers, or about €70–120K ARR. Subscriptions alone are unlikely to sustain a Spain-only business.

### Gaps
- I found no finance-category-specific RevenueCat conversion numbers (the full report could not be fetched), no data on EUR price points or willingness to pay in Spain or the EU after 2017, and no demographic breakdown of PFM users in Spain.

## 5. Trust: willingness to connect bank accounts, and open banking adoption

### Takeaway
Spaniards say they are willing to share data in exchange for clear benefits (70% in PwC 2025). They trust banks (55%) much more than fintechs, and fewer than about 10% have actually used open-banking services. Spain's open-banking use was 9.8% of adults in 2021, with a forecast of about 41% by 2027. Bizum's dominance slows open-banking payments.

### Cited Findings
- PwC/Strategy& Payments & Open Banking Survey 2025 (more than 5,500 interviews in 11 European countries): Spain leads, with 70% willing to share personal data in exchange for rewards, extra services or personalized offers. Trust in traditional banks as data custodians is 55%, up 37 points since 2018. Spain also leads Europe in digital payments, with 79% preferring cards and wallets over cash — [PwC press release 2025](https://www.pwc.es/es/sala-prensa/notas-prensa/2025/espana-cabeza-europa-adopcion-medios-pago-digitales.html); [PwC survey page](https://www.pwc.es/es/financiero/payments-open-banking-survey.html); [Capital](https://capital.es/economia/mercados-e-inversion/espana-lidera-europa-en-pagos-digitales-el-79-prefiere-tarjetas-y-wallets-frente-al-efectivo/156472)
- Deloitte (older, over 1,000 banked Spaniards): Spaniards trust big banks (53%) and large retailers (38%) most with their data. They distrust Big Tech (51%), telcos (50%) and fintechs (46%) — [Deloitte – Banca abierta](https://www.deloitte.com/es/es/services/consulting/analysis/banca-abierta.html)
- Fewer than 10% of Spanish consumers have tried open-banking-based services, although more than 60% say they are interested (no primary source or date given) — [Computing.es](https://www.computing.es/mundo-digital/la-confianza-clave-para-que-el-open-banking-despegue-en-espana/)
- In ubimia's own operations (not a representative survey; credit context, Aug 2026), 80–90% of loan applicants authorize temporary bank-data access. People connect their accounts when the benefit is immediate — [Dircom/ubimia](https://www.dircom.org/2026/08/31/8-de-cada-10-solicitantes-comparte-sus-datos-bancarios-para-conseguir-mejores-condiciones/)
- Spain's open-banking use was 9.8% of adults in 2021, forecast at about 41% by 2027. Interest is about 60% in Spain, against 57% in France and 50% in the Netherlands (2021 analysis) — [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/Insights/2024/four-european-takes-open-banking.html); [Yapily – Spain](https://www.yapily.com/blog/open-banking-spain-instant-payments) (snippet-level)
- Spain had 171 TPPs in Q1 2025, 157 of them cross-border (Konsentus via Yapily). Italy and Spain had the most passported TPPs in Europe in Q1 2023 — [Yapily – Spain](https://www.yapily.com/blog/open-banking-spain-instant-payments); [Statista – TPP registrations by country](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1214254/number-of-open-banking-third-party-registrations-in-europe-by-country)
- Europe-wide: about 64M open-banking users was a projection for 2024, with no methodology given. 2022 brought the first quarterly fall in European TPP registrations (Konsentus/Statista) — [Yapily – Europe overview](https://www.yapily.com/blog/open-banking-in-europe); [Statista – TPP registrations Europe](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1214241/number-of-open-banking-third-party-registrations-in-europe)
- A Spanish forecast puts open-banking penetration at about 90% by 2029 (vendor forecast, low reliability) — [Semla](https://www.getsemla.com/blog/spain-set-to-reach-90-open-banking-penetration-by-2029)

### Inferences
- Trust is not a hard blocker if the value is concrete and immediate, as the 80–90% consent rate in lending shows. For a passive budgeting app with a vague benefit, the main barrier will be distrust of fintechs (46% distrust) and inertia. Partnering with a bank or using bank co-branding could reduce that friction.

### Gaps
- I found no measured (not forecast) 2025–2026 count of open-banking users in Spain or Europe, no Konsentus or Open Banking Europe API-call volumes for Spain, and no Banco de España data on PSD2 account-information service (AIS) usage.

## 6. Monetization models that work in PFM, and the Mint shutdown

### Takeaway
Free PFM paid for by ads and referrals (Mint) was not viable on its own and was folded into a lead-generation engine (Credit Karma). The European models that survive monetize through financial-product brokerage: Finanzguru (insurance, broker, contracts) and Fintonic (loans and insurance). US premium subscriptions (YNAB, Monarch) work at USD 95–110 per year with an engaged niche.

### Cited Findings
- Intuit announced in Oct 2023 that Mint would close on 1 Jan 2024 and that users would move to Credit Karma. The stated reason was consolidation, "combining the money management product expertise and momentum of Mint with Credit Karma's scale" — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2125596/the-popular-mint-budgeting-tool-is-shutting-down.html)
- Mint had 3.6M MAU in 2021 (Bloomberg, cited in coverage). It made money from ads and referrals. Commentators say Intuit preferred Credit Karma because of its higher revenue (an interpretation, not an Intuit statement). Credit Karma did not offer monthly and category budgets at launch — [NBC Miami / CNBC syndication](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/business/money-report/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-to-the-disappointment-of-loyal-users-this-is-the-peril-of-relying-on-free-services/3153432/); [Millennial Money Veteran](https://millennialmoneyveteran.beehiiv.com/p/37-mint-the-budget-app-is-shutting); [Slashdot](https://news.slashdot.org/story/23/11/02/2042229/mint-is-shutting-down-and-its-pushing-users-toward-credit-karma?sdsrc=prev)
- Finanzguru's model: more than 100K of its 1.5M users bought brokerage, financial or insurance products in the app, and annualized revenue passed €10M in 2022 — [PayPal newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2023-04-04-PayPal-Ventures-Invests-in-Finanzguru)
- Fintonic's model: a loan marketplace partnership with BBVA Consumer Finance, and first profitability in 2025 after leaving Latin America — [El Referente](https://elreferente.es/?p=16633); [Roams](https://roams.es/actualidad/finanzas/fintonic-nuevo-ceo-banco-santander-eneko-fonseca/)
- Monarch's subscription model grew about 20x in subscribers in the year after Mint closed (third-party estimate). Estimated revenue is about USD 12.6M, and the company was valued at about USD 850M in May 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/monarch-money/); [Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/competitive-landscape-personal-finance-and-budgeting-apps-2026/source/7)
- Subscription apps overall are shifting away from pure subscriptions toward hybrid monetization (RevenueCat 2025) — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions)

### Inferences
- A viable Taimo model in Spain probably needs a hybrid: freemium plus premium, and an affiliate marketplace (energy, telco, insurance and loan switching from detected recurring expenses), or B2B2C licensing of categorization to banks. Selling data is legally and reputationally hard under GDPR and PSD2 (my inference; not researched here).

### Gaps
- I found no unit economics (CAC, revenue per referral) for Spanish PFM affiliate models, and nothing on how Spain's "Ley de Servicios de Atención a la Clientela" or GDPR rules affect data monetization.

## 7. Trends: banks building in categorization, and AI finance assistants

### Takeaway
The big Spanish and neo banks already provide automatic categorization, forecasting and budgets for free. BBVA does this across banks via aggregation and uses AI, and Revolut and N26 offer budgets and analytics. That takes away the basic value of a standalone tracker. What a standalone app can still offer is a neutral multi-bank view and AI-driven guidance and actions.

### Cited Findings
- BBVA Spain: the app and website automatically categorize transactions (expense, income, savings/investment), *including other banks' transactions if the customer asks*, and learn from user corrections. BBVA offers forecasts of upcoming transactions based on recurring payments and detects fixed costs such as subscriptions from a catalog plus statistical analysis. ### Inferences
- A user with only one main bank, such as BBVA or Revolut, already gets free categorization. Taimo's addressable segment is multi-bank users, couples or households, and users who want proactive guidance, such as AI insights and "you can save €X by switching Y". Bank aggregation (BBVA already aggregates other banks) reduces even the multi-bank advantage.
